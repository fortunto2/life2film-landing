Christmas is the day most people film the most and edit the least. Presents, the table, the walk,
the grandparents on a video call. By the evening there are sixty clips and no energy for a
timeline. The montage that gets watched is two minutes long and made the same night.

## Shoot for the edit, lightly

Nobody wants to be the person filming. Five habits that cost nothing:

- **Hold each shot for five seconds.** The first second is always shaky; the edit needs the other
  four.
- **Film the faces, not the gifts.** The reaction is the film. The box is a cutaway.
- **One wide shot of the room** with everyone in it, early.
- **Thirty seconds of the table** before anyone sits down.
- **Something outside**: the street, the walk, the light. It gives the film a breath.

## Three ways to cut it

### One tap, the same evening

In [Life2Film](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761656888?pt=128499174&ct=blog-christmas&mt=8), spin the
calendar to the 25th and choose **Just a film**, or the day's summary row if you want the date as a
title. Pick a track. The app scores every clip for faces, motion and sharpness, keeps the moments
that carry the day, and cuts them on the beat. Thirty seconds by default, up to three minutes. If
it kept too much of the kitchen, say "more of the kids, skip the kitchen" and it re-cuts.

In December the **New Year** preset is also open: a family cut with a "New Year" title, meant for
the stretch from Christmas to the first of January as one film.

Nothing is uploaded. Christmas footage is about as personal as footage gets, and the analysis runs
on the phone, so there is no account and no server with the family on it.

### Twenty minutes, in an editor

Pick the song first. Open on a face, build with the room, peak at the presents or the table, end
on something quiet. Cut on the beat; any editor with beat markers will show them, and our
[BPM detector](/tools/bpm-detector/) exports a grid for Final Cut, Premiere and Resolve. Keep it under
two minutes. Nobody rewatches the four-minute one.

### The whole holiday, in order

For the family archive rather than the group chat: set the period to the week and choose
**Chronicle**. Every clip in the order it was shot, trimmed to its best seconds, with the dates on
screen, up to ten minutes, no music. It is the film the grandparents want, and it is the one
nobody makes because it looks like work. It is one tap.

## Christmas video ideas beyond the day

- **The December film.** Set the period to the month. Tree-buying, the school concert, the lights,
  the day itself. The summary row titles it "December".
- **The year, before the year ends.** The [year in review](/blog/year-in-review-video-iphone/)
  guide covers the recap, the chronicle and the Instagram version.
- **The same table, every year.** Cut only the dinner shots from each year's Christmas, oldest
  first, no music. Thirty seconds that get longer every year.
- **Video call grandparents.** Screen-record the call for a minute. It is a clip like any other,
  and it is the one you will want.

## Posting it

If it is going on Instagram or TikTok, render without music and add a track from their library at
posting time, or the audio may be muted. For the family chat, music in the file is fine. For a TV
at the grandparents', pick the horizontal shape; Life2Film follows the footage's own majority unless
you ask for vertical.
