The first year produces more video than any other year of a life, and it is filmed by the two most
tired people in the house. By the first birthday there are three or four hundred clips. The montage
everyone wants, two minutes from hospital to cake, is the one nobody has the evening to make.

## Two films, not one

Make both. They are different things.

**The recap**: ninety seconds to two minutes, one song, the milestones. First smile, first food, the
first time sitting up, crawling, the first steps if they came. Date captions at each jump, because
"when did she start walking" is a question this film should answer.

**The chronicle**: every clip, in order, trimmed to its best seconds, up to ten minutes, no music.
The one you will watch in fifteen years. Nobody makes it because it looks like a weekend of work.

## What to keep in the recap

- **The firsts**, obviously. One clip each, three to four seconds.
- **The same spot, different months.** The same chair, the same blanket. Growth is visible when the
  background holds still.
- **Faces reacting to the baby.** The grandparents' first visit beats most footage of the baby.
- **Sounds.** The first laugh deserves its own three seconds with the music ducked.
- **One clip per month, minimum**, so no month is missing.

Leave out the clips that are really photos: the baby asleep, filmed for twenty seconds. One is
plenty.

## Keeping the dates

Burn the month into the frame at each change: "March", "April". It is the difference between a
montage and a record. Any editor can do it with a title per clip; it is tedious, which is why it
rarely happens.

## Making it on the phone

In [Life2Film](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761656888?pt=128499174&ct=blog-baby&mt=8), set a
**custom period** from the birth date to the birthday and choose the summary row for the recap: it
scores every clip for faces, sharpness and motion, spreads the picks across the twelve months rather
than clustering on the weeks you filmed most, and cuts to the track you choose. The default is a
minute, with 30, 60 and 120 second options. The **Travel** preset is worth knowing here too: it was
built for day-by-day trips, but its one trick, a caption at every change of day, is exactly what a
first-year film wants.

For the chronicle, same period, **Chronicle** preset. Everything in order, up to ten minutes, dates
on screen, no music.

Faces are grouped on the device, so a film about one child in a house with two is something you
steer with words: "more of the baby, less of the dog" re-cuts in seconds.

## Why on the phone matters here

A year of a newborn is the most private footage a person will ever hold. Every automatic montage
service so far has needed it uploaded first. Life2Film's analysis runs on the iPhone: no account,
no server, nothing leaves the phone. [What the alternatives do](/blog/where-your-footage-goes/) is
worth a read before you hand a year of your child to any app, including ours.

## Songs

Slow build, a lift around two thirds in, under three minutes. Put the first steps, or the first
laugh, on the lift. If you are posting it, use the platform's own music library at posting time;
for the family, anything you love.
