By December a phone holds a year: a few hundred clips, most never watched twice. A year in review
video is the one piece of editing almost everyone wants to have done and almost nobody does,
because picking two minutes out of six hours is the job. There are three shapes it can take, and
each one has a reason to exist.

## 1. The recap: two minutes, to music

The classic. Thirty to ninety moments, one song, cut on the beat, titled with the year. It is what
Instagram and Google Photos generate automatically, and the reason theirs feel generic is that the
selection is by date, not by what the clips contain.

Doing it by hand: pick one clip per month first, so the year is covered, then add the ten moments
that matter regardless of month. Order by date, not by quality. Put the song's lift on the best
one. Export at the shape of the majority of the footage.

In [Life2Film](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761656888?pt=128499174&ct=blog-year-review&mt=8), set the
period to the **year** and choose the summary row, which carries the year as its title. The default
length is a minute, with 30, 60 and 120 second options; the app scores every clip for sharpness,
faces and motion and spreads the picks across the year rather than clustering on the one trip you
filmed too much of. Pick a track; the cuts land on its beats.

## 2. The chronicle: everything, in order, no music

The one people do not think to make and then keep forever. Every clip of the year, in the order it
was shot, trimmed to its best seconds, up to ten minutes. No music, just the sound that was there.
It is not for posting. It is for the family, and for yourself in ten years.

Life2Film's **Chronicle** preset is this film. For a year it leads the list, because ten minutes of
everything is what a year is actually for. It keeps capture order, ignores the beat grid, and can
carry the dates on screen so you know which week you are watching.

## 3. The one for Instagram: fifteen seconds, vertical

The recap's younger sibling. Fifteen to thirty seconds, 9:16, hard on the beat, one caption. The
rule is one moment per second, so fifteen moments. Anything slower reads as a slideshow.

Choose **Reels** in Life2Film and it forces the vertical shape regardless of the footage, starts at
fifteen seconds, and cuts on the beat of the track you pick. Post it natively; Instagram's library
music is the safe choice for the audio, so you may want to render without music and add it there.

## New Year, specifically

A New Year recap is a year in review with a deadline. Life2Film's **New Year** preset is grey for
eleven months and unlocks in December: a family cut, a "New Year" title, and the same lengths as the
birthday film. Make the year chronicle in the quiet days before, the recap on the 30th, and the
Reel while the party is on. All three come from the same footage.

## Songs for a year recap

Pick something with a clear lift at about two thirds of its length, and put the year's best moment
there. Avoid songs with a long intro; the first cut should land within two seconds. If you are
posting to Instagram or TikTok, use their library at posting time, or the audio may be removed.
Our free [BPM detector](/tools/bpm-detector/) shows where the beats are in any track, for the by-hand
version.
