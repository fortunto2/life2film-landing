A birthday leaves forty clips on a phone: the cake from three angles, a song nobody finished, a
minute of someone looking for the lighter. The montage people want is ninety seconds, with the
faces, the candles and the laugh, cut to one song. Here is how to get there, by hand or in one tap.

## What to keep

Pick in this order, and stop when you have enough:

1. **The reaction shots.** The face when the cake arrives beats the cake.
2. **The candles.** Both the blowing out and the second before.
3. **One wide shot of the room.** Where it was, who was there.
4. **Short bursts of talking**, two or three seconds each, not whole speeches.
5. **The leftovers**: dancing, kids under the table, the dog with a balloon.

Drop anything shaky in the first second, anything shot through a window, and every clip where the
subject is a phone screen.

## Structure that works

- **Open on the person**, not on the decorations. Three seconds, close.
- **Build with the room**: arrivals, hugs, the table filling up.
- **Peak on the candles.** Put the song's loudest moment here.
- **Fall away with the party**: the dancing, the mess, one quiet shot at the end.
- **Title at the start or the end**, not both: the name and the year is enough.

Ninety seconds is the right length for a family group chat. Two minutes if there were grandparents
and speeches. Nobody has ever asked for the four-minute version.

## Music

Choose the song before you cut, then cut to it. The edits should land on the beat, and the candles
should land where the song lifts. Any editor with beat markers can show you where the beats are;
our free [BPM detector](/tools/bpm-detector/) exports them for Final Cut, Premiere and Resolve. If
the film is going on Instagram, use a track from Instagram's own library at posting time, or it may
be muted.

## The one-tap version

In [Life2Film](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761656888?pt=128499174&ct=blog-birthday&mt=8), spin the
calendar to the day and choose **Happy birthday**. The preset sets a family-friendly cut, a
one-minute starting length with 30, 60 and 120 second options, and a "Happy birthday" title you can
edit. Pick a track and the cuts land on its beats; the shot with the candles is found by scoring
every clip for faces, motion and sharpness, not by you scrubbing through forty files.

If the first cut keeps the wrong things, say so: "shorter, more of the kids, skip the kitchen" and
it re-cuts in seconds. Everything runs on the phone, so the party footage is never uploaded.

One honest limit: the preset does not yet know whose birthday it is. Faces are grouped on the
device, but "the film about Anna" is a thing you steer with words, not a setting.

## Birthday montage ideas, when the day itself is thin

- **A year of them.** Set the period to the twelve months since the last birthday and pick the
  **Chronicle** preset: everything in the order it was filmed, up to ten minutes, no music, the
  dates on screen. It plays like a diary.
- **The same shot, every year.** If you have a tradition of filming the candles, cut only those,
  oldest first. Thirty seconds, no music, the ages as captions.
- **Messages from people who were not there.** Collect ten-second clips, drop them into one album,
  and run **Just a film** on the album.
